# Stripe card top-ups — focused design proposal

**Status: local synthetic-only implementation authorized after independent planning review.** The operator authorized code and tests for local synthetic payments, not Stripe account access, secret access, live processor requests, gateway changes, real payments, inference probes, export, new resources, commits, publication or deployment. No Stripe credentials are needed for offline work. Existing `PLAN.md` and historical evidence remain unchanged; the scoped intake privacy amendment governs newly authorized synthetic records. This is neither legal advice nor live acceptance evidence.

Current source implementation, passing local source/image checks and remaining real-provider/release gates are recorded in [the local packet](stripe-topup-local.md). Production proposals below are not live acceptance. The offline adapter deliberately accepts only a loopback fake, not actual Stripe endpoints or credentials; normal runtime remains disabled.

## 1. Direction, facts and unresolved policy

**User-selected direction:** card-first, small fixed increments, a fresh temporary processor reference rather than the account identifier, and 20-day token/provider-link expiry. Crypto, e-cash and anonymous credentials stay deferred.

**Proposed first product:** authenticated existing customers buy one-time prepaid inference credit through Stripe-hosted guest Checkout. Offer **$5 / $10 / $25 / $50 USD**, subject to final approval. No subscriptions, autoreload, reusable payment links, saved-card feature, public signup or unnecessary Stripe Customer objects. Credits are proposed for this seller's service only, not transferable or generally redeemable money. Those restrictions do not themselves establish regulatory or Stripe eligibility.

**Operator-selected refund scope:** refund/chargeback automation and ledger clawback are out of scope for the initial implementation. The operator accepts absorbing refunded/disputed purchase value and associated losses rather than recovering credit from accounts. This does not waive applicable customer rights or establish a refund window/promise. Any necessary provider-side handling remains a separate manual operation; this packet neither performs nor automates it. Preserve the proposed 20-day unlinking without a hidden refund/dispute retention extension. Before-funding evidence validation remains required: absorbing losses does not authorize funding an already-refunded or disputed payment.

**Existing commercial facts:** README and gateway guidance state a $5 minimum and 30% inference markup. Proposed interpretation: a $10 purchase adds $10 of balance, spent at the gateway's marked-up inference prices; do not also subtract 30% on funding. The ledger does not calculate inference prices.

**Operator-confirmed location:** San Francisco, California, United States. Business entity and customer sales regions remain unspecified. The operator explicitly deferred tax discussion/research for now; this is not a tax exemption or approval to activate real sales. Continue planning without making tax decisions, retaining applicable tax qualification as a launch checklist item.

**Launch decisions, not assumed acceptance:** business entity and sales regions; Stripe eligibility for this exact prepaid-credit model; currency denomination of existing balances; taxes and required customer-location/invoice evidence; gross charge versus credited amount; processor fees and FX; credit expiry/transfer/redemption terms; any required public refund terms and manual support procedure (automation/clawback deferred and losses accepted); accounting retention and deletion/legal obligations. Do not import Mullvad's jurisdiction, accounting period or 14-day refund policy. If legal or Stripe obligations require a longer provider-to-account link, stop for an explicit product/privacy decision: no hidden “open case” exception to 20 days.

These uncertainties **do not prevent the now-authorized offline synthetic implementation** of the accounting state machine. They do prevent real-data activation and publication of the notice below. Local fixture choices are USD denominations below, 20 days from reference issuance, a one-hour payable lifetime and 90 UTC days of synthetic sales retention. That finite fixture retention is not a chosen legal/business period for real sales. Payment schema setup remains explicit and synthetic-only; ordinary initialization/open/serve never implicitly enables it.

## 2. Evidence and repository fit

### Public sources checked for this draft

- [Mullvad no-logging policy](https://mullvad.net/en/help/no-logging-data-policy): the processor receives a token, not the account number; the token expires after 20 days. Stripe charge ID and time-of-day are deleted 20 days after payment, **not all account-linked sales history**. Payment/account/amount/currency/date records remain subject to accounting retention. Mullvad cites 14-day refunds and payment issues; its late-chargeback/replay implementation is not published. This is inspiration, not an equivalence or legality claim.
- [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses) includes stored-value products and says seller-maintained credits may be subject to limits. Obtain eligibility confirmation for the actual model and applicable country; “small increments” is not an exemption. Review the applicable [Services Agreement](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa) and [service/regional terms](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms), including receipts, taxes, refunds and disputes, with qualified advice where needed.
- [Checkout creation](https://docs.stripe.com/api/checkout/sessions/create) says omitting `customer_email` still asks the payer for email. [Guest customers](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/guest-customers) can be grouped by Stripe using card/email/phone even without a Customer object. Guest is not anonymous.
- [Stripe privacy](https://stripe.com/privacy) describes controller and/or processor roles and its own legal, fraud and financial retention. Our deletion cannot erase Stripe/card-network records or Stripe's ability to correlate purchases.
- [Fulfillment](https://docs.stripe.com/checkout/fulfillment), [webhooks](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) and [idempotency](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests) require duplicate/concurrency handling; events may be out of order, different events can represent one payment, and v1 request keys can be removed after at least 24 hours. A fresh delivery signature is not a fresh purchase.
- Use the supported [Stripe Go SDK](https://github.com/stripe/stripe-go), not hand-written webhook cryptography. Its current README documents automatic retries (two by default), telemetry and stderr logging. Explicitly configure zero network retries and `BackendConfig.EnableTelemetry = stripe.Bool(false)` on every used backend, independently of `OTEL_SDK_DISABLED`. Use the pinned SDK's supported no-output logger configuration and qualify Stripe-Notice handling separately: the current README says notices are always printed in agent environments, so the suppression environment variable alone is not proof. If supported controls cannot suppress raw notices in the actual runtime, record an SDK qualification blocker rather than claiming logs are off. Pin an exact stable module/API version after compatibility review, not an invented version in this document. [Stripe security guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/security/guide) treats PCI compliance as shared responsibility: hosted Checkout reduces scope, not all obligations.

Stock edits/deletion/schema changes/restore can bypass serialized guards and destroy replay evidence. Initial rules stay SDK nil/superuser-only where collections are used. Do not add RBAC, restrict trusted authority or disable SDK constraints to make the payment proof appear stronger.

Required sibling guidance, current SPEC/OVERALL_PLAN/full PRIVACY and historical `7ee4604:OVERALL_PLAN.md` were read. Historical hand-rolled webhook HMAC, account-number URLs, post-completion debits, browser gateway and anonymity claims are not this design. No gateway implementation or paid-balance integration is claimed.

## 3. Minimal purchase and Checkout flow

Proposed fixed host routes: `GET /topup` (form), `POST /topup/start` (explicit create action), `GET /topup/status` (local evidence), and `GET /topup/return` (same fixed destination for success/cancel). All are account-scoped through current SDK/session validation; the POST requires CSRF and stale-form protection. Reserve `/topup` and `/payments` against Lisp route ownership. `POST /payments/stripe/webhook` is the separate signature-authenticated route. No query-string selectors or public purchase-token lookup.

1. A Go-owned authenticated top-up page offers the four fixed choices. A CSRF-protected POST revalidates the current SDK account binding. The server maps a closed denomination to approved currency/charge/credit values; no client amount, account, return URL or metadata is authoritative. Keep stale-publication refusal and current secure-session rules. Issue a random form attempt value into the authenticated memory-only session with a ten-minute submission lifetime, capped by session expiry. Persist it only in the temporary purchase row when the POST creates that row. Resolve a retained attempt first, checking account and denomination equality; different inputs conflict. Only a still-live server-issued session attempt can create a missing row. This coalesces repeated POSTs without allowing old forms to recreate pruned purchases; restart invalidates unsubmitted forms. No purchase mapping is stored merely by viewing a form.
2. Under `Service.run`, allocate a **32-byte cryptographically random purchase reference**, independently random from account/key/session/sale identity. Persist the immutable purchase and a create-attempt claim before any Stripe call. Never derive the reference or SDK idempotency key from account material. One original reference means one potential payment; collision fails, never overwrites. The reference is a confidential mapping token, not payment authorization or proof of account ownership.
3. Outside the transaction, make **one** bounded SDK Checkout create attempt, with one stored independent random SDK idempotency key and immutable parameters. Set `mode=payment`, hosted UI, card-only, one quantity, server price/currency, automatic capture, `customer_creation=if_required`; omit `customer`, `customer_account`, `customer_email`, future-use setup, invoice creation and saved-payment options. Disable promotions, adjustable quantity, adaptive currency conversion and session recovery/after-expiration replacement. Keep tax behavior explicitly gated by the approved pricing/tax policy, not silently disabled for real sales. Qualify Link/wallet/dashboard defaults so the offered methods and saving behavior match the public product.
4. The only customer-specific value we deliberately send is the purchase reference in one Checkout reference/metadata field (proposed `client_reference_id`). Do not duplicate it into descriptions, PaymentIntent metadata, receipts or URLs. Stripe's own Session/PaymentIntent IDs are provider identifiers, not credentials for local account access. Fixed success/cancel URLs contain no account, purchase reference or `{CHECKOUT_SESSION_ID}`. No email/address is copied from our auth state.
5. Validate the SDK's returned hosted Checkout HTTPS origin and bound Session before a Go-owned 303 redirect. Persist only the Session ID, not the hosted URL. Set `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on the departure/return surfaces; qualify the existing CSP with this navigation rather than broadly enabling Stripe scripts. The opaque Stripe-hosted URL necessarily enters the browser/history and Stripe boundary. Full PAN/CVC go directly to Stripe, never through intake. SDK/webhook objects may transiently include email, address, card summaries and risk fields; discard them rather than saving/logging full objects.
6. A fixed return page shows only authenticated local status/current balance; visiting it, refreshing it or pressing “check status” cannot create a Session, charge, retry a mutation or establish funding. A return/cancel redirect is not paid/canceled evidence. Restart loses browser sessions and their private purchase selector; the current minimal website does not reconstruct it on fresh login or expose an account/global payment list. A later account-owned recovery view would require its own scoped design.

**Crash/unknown-create rule:** mark the attempt before sending; a crash there may strand an unsent attempt. A lost response may instead hide a created payable Session. Neither permits replaying create, even with the same SDK key; Stripe's retention is not permanent. Repeated form POSTs return existing state, never issue a replacement Session. A signed event or separately authorized read-only provider lookup may bind the one original Session if all checks below pass. Unknown attempts must not be automatically re-created after 24 hours or restart. After the short payable lifetime, the user can deliberately begin a new purchase with a warning that the earlier payment outcome may remain unknown. Do not infer safe retry from local absence.

This deliberately trades some checkout availability for a small, non-replaying implementation; no durable queue, retry worker or generic operations framework. **Transport qualification gate:** zero SDK retries does not alone prove one wire dispatch. Go HTTP transport can replay eligible requests on reused-connection failures when the SDK supplies an idempotency header and replayable body. Before freezing implementation, qualify actual wire dispatches under those faults and reject redirects through supported HTTP client configuration. If supported controls cannot meet the proposed no-replay contract, stop for an explicit policy decision rather than equating one SDK call with one network attempt.

## 4. Exact storage and clocks (proposal requiring approval)

Use two financial tables plus one synthetic feature-version singleton as parameterized SQL tables in PocketBase's existing **data database**, owned by the ledger service, not a second database/service. All new payment tables must use SQLite `WITHOUT ROWID`, including sales: a TEXT primary key alone still leaves a hidden sequential integer rowid, which can reveal insertion-order correlations after purchase deletion. Qualify actual DDL and hidden-key absence, not just visible fields. This does not remove physical storage-order/WAL/snapshot correlation. No generic payment CRUD or SDK record timestamps are needed. Preserve unrestricted native admin authority over the underlying system. Every amount is canonical nonnegative uint64 decimal **TEXT**, validated in Go with checked arithmetic and SQL type/shape constraints; no float NumberField, SQL monetary arithmetic or coerced string amounts. Time deadlines are checked signed integer epoch milliseconds; retained dates are validated `YYYY-MM-DD` UTC text.

| Table | Minimal fields and invariants | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| `payment_purchases` | `purchase_ref TEXT PRIMARY KEY`; `form_attempt TEXT UNIQUE`; `account_id TEXT` exact ledger FK; `currency TEXT` closed approved value; `charge_minor TEXT`; `credit_units TEXT`; `issued_ms INTEGER`; immutable `unlink_at INTEGER`; `checkout_expires_at INTEGER`; `create_key TEXT UNIQUE`; `create_state TEXT` (`prepared/attempted/known/unknown`); nullable `stripe_session_id TEXT UNIQUE`, `stripe_payment_intent_id TEXT UNIQUE`, `sale_id TEXT UNIQUE`; `funding_state TEXT` (`unfunded/funded/blocked`). No raw payload, event log, email, name, IP, address, card fingerprint/last4, failure text or hosted URL. | Entire row, including every index value, is deleted at eligibility cutoff by maintenance. No renewal from payment, retry, support or dispute. |
| `payment_sales` | independent random `sale_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY`; `account_id TEXT`; `sale_date TEXT` UTC charge-creation date for the verified paid/captured Charge (date convention qualified below); `currency TEXT`; `gross_minor TEXT`; `credit_units TEXT`. No provider ID, reference, create key, exact timestamp, balance-after, browser/form/session ID or FK to a provider record. Account linkage is intentional accounting retention, not an anonymized record. | Explicit legal/business-approved sales period `R_sales`, still unresolved. No production default of forever. Synthetic fixtures use 90 UTC days; they do not choose the legal period. Delete under that policy without altering current balance. |
| `payment_meta` | singleton feature schema version, synthetic-only marker and finite fixture sales-retention configuration; no customer/payment identity. | Explicit feature setup only; ordinary serve never creates or resets it. |

**Implementation simplification:** omit the proposed provider-only tombstone table. Live-purchase unique identities protect overlapping eligible purchases; canonical Session-to-reference/create-window validation, no rebinding/reference reuse and mandatory existing eligible purchase prevent an old object creating another effect after pruning. A permanent marker adds retention without a distinct invariant here. The tests must cover those assumptions directly.

Indexes: purchase `(account_id, unlink_at)` and `(unlink_at)`; sales `(sale_date)` plus account index only if the approved query needs it; primary/unique indexes as above. Required fields are NOT NULL except the explicitly nullable purchase bindings; money must be positive for these sales. Purchase account FKs use ON DELETE RESTRICT; retained sales preserve their exact account text, not a cascading live-account dependency. No cascade deletes replay state when an account is removed. Sales retention must cover at least the live purchase-link interval; a shorter required policy needs a revised consistency contract. Pin column/null/state consistency and restart validation in the migration packet. `funded` requires the corresponding independent sale while the purchase remains; never “repair” missing evidence by funding again.

**Synthetic denomination contract:** one USD is 1,000,000 ledger microunits, one cent is 10,000; $5/$10/$25/$50 gives charge cents `500/1000/2500/5000` and credit units `5000000/10000000/25000000/50000000`. All multiplication and balance addition are checked; convert to SDK signed integer only after range checking. This is a proposed unit declaration, **not proof that existing real balances are USD**. Before activation approve that declaration and tax/fee handling. If the actual tax policy needs extra monetary fields or identifying evidence, revise this schema/privacy proposal first; do not stash it in free-form JSON.

### Cutoff definition

- Use the existing transaction clock `now = max(SQLite wall time, persisted high-water mark)`, with checked arithmetic. **Proposed 20 days starts at local token issuance:** `unlink_at = issued_ms + 20 × 24h`. This is an exact duration, not calendar-month arithmetic. It differs from Mullvad's payment-aged charge-ID rule; here the earlier token deadline also ends the provider link. A later payment does not get a fresh 20 days. Approve this interpretation explicitly.
- Proposed Checkout expiry is **one hour after issuance**, set server-side on create; claim/send creation promptly and no later than five minutes after issuance. Stripe documents a 30-minute–24-hour Session lifetime; qualify this chosen one-hour setting and clock skew through its SDK/API contract. Expired unpaid sessions cannot restart or recover into a fresh payable Session under the old reference.
- Provider Session creation time is immutable seconds converted with checked arithmetic. Require it within the original create window (allow at most 60 seconds of clock skew), the bound reference/Session to match, and `now < session.created_ms + 20 days`. Reject unexpected creation/expiry values. Funding also requires `now < unlink_at`. Never use webhook event creation/delivery time to extend either deadline, or PaymentIntent creation time as proof of capture time. For the minimal schema, `sale_date` is proposed as the UTC date of the canonical successful Charge's `created` value, after verifying it is paid/captured and belongs to that PaymentIntent. This is a charge-creation date, not an invented capture timestamp: delayed capture or 3DS can cross a date boundary. Accounting/tax review must approve that date convention or require a revised authoritative payment-date source before real sales; do not silently substitute webhook receipt time.
- No provider-only tombstone survives unlinking. Missing/pruned purchase references never authorize funding, and a canonical old Session cannot match a fresh purchase's immutable create window. Neither event freshness nor later metadata editing extends that window.
- Forward clock jumps can unlink early and strand paid funding; backwards jumps cannot reopen eligibility after committed pruning. Long downtime can delay physical row deletion. The watermark is not rollback-resistant storage or trusted external time.

## 5. Verified payment → one atomic funding effect

### Webhook and evidence boundary

Add one fixed reserved Go-owned webhook route outside customer CSRF/session middleware and Lisp publication availability, while preserving CSRF everywhere customer actions require it. It is authenticated by Stripe's supported signature verification, **not** by the shared accounting key. Existing `/credits`, `/balance` and reservation endpoints retain their shared key unchanged.

Use bounded raw request bytes before JSON transformation (proposed 256 KiB webhook cap, qualified with fixtures), the Stripe signature header and runtime-only webhook secret with the SDK's timestamp tolerance. Do not disable API-version checks or signature freshness to make fixtures pass. Reject oversized/invalid/old-signature input with a closed 4xx; valid irrelevant events get an inert 2xx. No raw body/event object is persisted, logged or queued. The local implementation supports Checkout completion/async-success notifications for canonical reads. Standalone refund/dispute notifications are inert; canonical Charge refund/dispute evidence is still required before funding. Any additional notification lookup/handling is separately scoped, not an implicit refund automation feature. Card/3DS processing is not paid evidence.

For a relevant notification, use supported typed decoding and bounded SDK **read** calls outside transactions to retrieve the canonical Session, its PaymentIntent and successful Charge as needed. Validate expected Stripe account/context, configured test/live mode, pinned API version, `mode=payment`, original reference/Session relationship, one approved line item/quantity, currency, exact gross amount and amount received, `payment_status=paid`, PaymentIntent success, and Charge `status=succeeded`, `paid=true`, `captured=true`, exact `amount_captured` and `amount_refunded=0`. The [Charge contract](https://docs.stripe.com/api/charges/object) makes `paid` alone insufficient and `refunded=false` insufficient to exclude partial refunds. Exclude `no_payment_required`, authorization-only, pending/processing/3DS, mismatched totals/currencies, refunds or disputes already observed. No funding solely from a browser field, `checkout.session.completed` name, a signed but stale snapshot or an unrelated `payment_intent.succeeded` event. Multiple notifications converge on the **same Session and PaymentIntent**, not event ID.

If the create response was lost, binding is allowed only for the still-live original `attempted/unknown` row, matching reference, immutable create/expiry window and exact server purchase terms. There must be no different already-bound Session/PaymentIntent. Persist the binding under the same gate and uniqueness constraints; never rebind or create an account/purchase from provider metadata. A provider payment appearing under two purchases is a conflict, not two receipts. No app path edits provider references or attaches an existing PaymentIntent to a new purchase.

### Funding transaction

Add a narrow typed service method, proposed `Service.FundStripePurchase`, inside `internal/ledger`. Do **not** expose `core.App` to the Stripe adapter for accounting or call the HTTP `/credits` endpoint. Reuse existing checked-money/account/hold helpers with the smallest extraction needed, not nested `Service.run` or a generic public transaction callback.

Inside one existing serialized `Service.run` / supported PocketBase transaction, using only `tx.app`/`tx.query`:

1. Re-read the original purchase, clock and bound canonical identities; recheck eligibility and immutable expected amounts. Refresh the effective transaction clock before the funding decision; a remote read completed before expiry does not preserve eligibility. Expired/missing references never authorize new effects, even if provider evidence is valid. Cutoff is evaluated at the serialized decision, not a guaranteed wall-clock fsync completion deadline: PocketBase/OS commit can stall after that decision. Preserve this existing transaction limitation rather than claim hard real-time deletion or abort.
2. Resolve both unique provider identities and the purchase funding state. A matching retained funded purchase returns its known original funding status; inconsistent identities/state fail closed. This receipt says credit was added, **not that it remains available now**.
3. Load the exact existing account and validate total/active holds through the existing helpers. Do not silently provision or switch accounts. Missing/corrupt account or overflow rolls back without a marker; disabled spending does not itself erase money paid or prevent crediting an existing valid account.
4. Checked-add the approved `credit_units`, insert the independent sale row, and set the purchase `funded`/`sale_id` in the **same transaction**. One commit owns all effects. No Stripe, rendering, telemetry or remote refund call inside it.
5. Only authoritative completion establishes funded. Preserve unknown commit outcome and later response failure separately. A webhook 2xx acknowledges processed/duplicate/ineligible delivery, not necessarily customer funding. On eligible transient lookup/storage/unknown-completion failure return a closed retryable 5xx; Stripe may redeliver, safely against the same identities. Do not acknowledge eligible work early without durable handling and then rely on an unimplemented queue.

Stripe payments **do not create `ledger_credits` entries**. Therefore provider references cannot survive via generic credit idempotency keys, original `balance_after`, terminal records or renewed retry keys. Manual `/credits` behavior is unchanged. Never “repair” a payment by issuing a new generic credit key.

### Why pruning does not reopen a paid event

Before cutoff, unique Session + PaymentIntent and purchase state serialize all concurrent/duplicate/out-of-order funding attempts with the balance write. Event-ID dedup alone would not do this. After purchase cutoff, the mapping is ineligible even if maintenance has not deleted it; lookup cannot recreate it. After its deletion, an old reference has no account authority. New forms mint independent references; old forms cannot restore a row. After mapping pruning, the immutable Session creation-age/create-window gate also rejects reuse of that Session under another reference. A new event ID, fresh webhook signature, delayed delivery, changed retry timestamp or expired Stripe request key cannot change those facts. Canonical Session→PaymentIntent binding prevents using a different event type as another payment.

The proof assumes original durable state, supported Stripe object relationships, no application reference reuse/rebinding and no trusted-admin corruption. Test those assumptions. Native SQL/schema edits, provider-admin mutation, restoring old state or copying spendable ledgers can break the proof; no new authority gate or financial restore guarantee is introduced.

## 6. Unlinking, alternate joins and honest deletion

At `now >= unlink_at`, all customer/provider resolution and funding paths refuse use of the purchase mapping. Extend the existing joined maintenance owner with bounded deletion batches through `Service.run`; delete the **whole** expired purchase row, not just `account_id`. On startup, purge overdue mappings before enabling payment/customer-payment admission, in bounded transactions; do not reset state or call Stripe. Failed purge closes payment admission with a typed failure, not accounting corruption repair. Qualify deletion backlog/capacity; there is no hard physical-deletion-at-the-millisecond claim during outage or backlog.

Explicit inventory for tests/review:

- Remove reference, account binding, exact issuance/expiry, SDK create key, form token, Session/PaymentIntent and sale-ID bridge together. No application secondary table/session cache or automatic export may preserve that bridge intentionally. Request/SDK memory can retain in-flight values until handling finishes; immediate memory erasure is not promised. Trusted operator copies remain outside the deletion guarantee. No sales-history page is proposed; any later account sales view must not expose a permanent provider receipt ID.
- Sales retain **account + UTC sales date + exact amount/currency**, deliberately. Repeated purchases, low volume and those fields can correlate with Stripe records. Removing explicit keys is not statistical unlinkability.
- Do not reuse a provider ID/reference in `ledger_credits`, reservation keys, generic terminal rows, native auth fields, website publication/drafts or diagnostic text. Existing independently generated reservation/credit timestamps can still correlate with balance/activity, even without a direct payment key.
- The current account schema deliberately omits SDK per-record timestamps; new ordinary payment tables also omit automatic `created`/`updated`. Inventory actual PocketBase schema/settings/auth fields and native admin-added fields: never assume merely omitting a display column removed a join. Avoid duplicate SDK records or generated invoice IDs that link retained sales to Stripe.
- SQLite WAL, free/deleted pages, optional native snapshots, downloaded archives, browser history, Stripe API request history and provider/card-network records remain separate persistence boundaries. Logical deletion is **not secure erasure**. No VACUUM/key-rotation/backup system is added as an unverified erasure promise. There are no scheduled backups by default; trusted admins can manually copy or restore old mappings. Tinfoil's accepted managed-key custody permits platform disk decryption and encrypted volumes do not prevent rollback.
- No account-linked payment diagnostics, access logs, SDK logs, raw exceptions, webhook spools, support bundles or new metrics. Existing logs-off/AuthAlert controls and [forbidden collection](../PRIVACY.md#forbidden-collection-and-defaults) remain. Stripe's own retention and guest grouping are not controlled by these settings.

There is **no automatic open-case retention extension**. If a required tax/audit/refund process cannot work with this deletion contract, revise it explicitly before live acceptance, rather than quietly preserving a mapping elsewhere.

## 7. Reconciliation, refunds and disputes

**Status is observational:** authenticated local status may return `unconfirmed`, `awaiting_payment`, `support_required`, `funded` or `unavailable_after_unlink`, based only on retained evidence. It must not turn “not found” into “not charged.” Customer status performs no Stripe mutation or automatic retry. Keep the existing account snapshot separate because it may expire holds.

**Reconciliation is an explicit operation, not a status side effect:** an authorized operator may perform bounded provider read-only retrieval for a known original attempt while its link is eligible; it may feed the same atomic funding function for an already-captured payment. No new charge/create/confirm/capture or replacement key is issued. Unknown create outcomes with no Session ID require deliberate bounded provider lookup, never an automatic broad customer search or retained payload dump. After cutoff, even verified late payment cannot auto-credit an account or reconstruct the mapping from metadata/date/amount. Handle provider-side resolution manually under an approved policy; this can mean a payment was captured without usable credit. Launch must approve that customer remedy and operational risk.

**Refunds are not reservation refunds.** Existing `Service.Refund` only releases an inference hold; it does not refund Stripe or subtract prepaid balance. Do not use it, negative `Credit`, stale native balance edits or a manufactured reservation as a payment-refund implementation.

Proposed minimal first lane: **no automatic clawback, negative balance, account suspension or hold cancellation** from refunds/disputes. Before funding, observed refund/dispute evidence blocks that purchase; ordinary out-of-order success events cannot clear the block. Refund objects can be pending, requires_action, succeeded, failed or canceled; a refund notification alone never establishes returned funds. Relevant refund evidence, including unknown future statuses, conservatively retains blocking pending approved handling; qualify card-only applicability rather than assuming all statuses are reachable. Proposed conservative pre-funding behavior is `blocked` with customer status `support_required` on relevant refund evidence, without automatic unblocking from later failed/canceled refund events or out-of-order success events. Approval must explicitly choose whether such purchases remain blocked for manual remedy or get a separately specified verified transition; this draft does not invent automatic unblocking. Pin each status and notification case in fixtures. After funding, provider notifications do not undo a confirmed credit. Verification and a concurrent refund can race across the network/DB boundary; we cannot make Stripe and SQLite one transaction. Already-spent credit cannot be recovered by uint64 subtraction; held credit cannot safely be taken from under existing reservations. A later dispute may reverse provider proceeds regardless of local unlinking.

The operator selected loss absorption: no refund/chargeback automation or ledger clawback in the initial implementation. Any required manual provider-side handling is outside this packet; credited/spent value and associated losses are absorbed, with no ledger debit, hold cancellation or retained post-cutoff mapping for recovery. A serialized adjustment workflow is not part of this plan and would require separate authorization. Public refund/support terms and applicable rights still require launch qualification; no manual provider mutation is authorized by this document. Required legal refunds cannot simply be refused because a link expired. Stripe can still identify/refund/dispute its payment after unlinking, but we can no longer reliably find or debit the credited account. Do not promise post-unlink credit recovery. Do not retain prompts, inference history, model/token usage or account activity as dispute evidence; accept the evidentiary limits and loss exposure explicitly. Any new support-supplied relinking data or legal retention exception needs separate approval and disclosure.

## 11. Independent review disposition

The first fresh Astra review found two design gaps, not deployed leaks: hidden SQLite rowids could preserve an exact sale/tombstone bridge, and Stripe SDK telemetry was not explicitly disabled. This revision requires `WITHOUT ROWID`, hidden-key negative tests, every-backend SDK telemetry disablement and multi-request wire/notice tests. Parent independently reproduced the hidden-rowid join with disposable in-memory SQLite and its rejection using `WITHOUT ROWID`; the public SDK README confirms telemetry and notice defaults. These checks are not application acceptance or proof of physical erasure. The review also requested a third-table necessity decision and explicit failed/canceled-refund handling; both are now recorded above, with conservative blocking proposed rather than silently adopting an automatic remedy. Two nonblocking qualifications are now explicit: HTTP transport retries/redirects must be tested independently of SDK retries, and `requires_action`/unknown refund statuses retain conservative blocking. The implementation contract resolves the third-table question by omitting redundant provider-only markers, retaining only the required feature-version singleton alongside purchases and sales. No application acceptance evidence follows from these reviews.
